1827 to 2024: 200 years of scientific collaboration

How has scientific collaboration evolved in astronomy? This project mines 200 years of astronomical papers from the NASA ADS API, tracking how authorship shifted from solitary researchers with telescopes to massive international collaborations.

Average Authors per Paper

The 1960 inflection point marks the shift from solo research (about 1 author) to collaborative science. By 2024, averaging roughly 12 authors per paper. The steep curve mirrors the rise of Big Science.

Key Findings

2012 Original Analysis

  • 1827-1960: Authorship remained flat at roughly 1 author per paper
  • 1960+: Sharp acceleration in co-authorship across all journals
  • By 2012: Single-author papers just 6 percent of the total (down from roughly 100 percent in 1827)
  • Largest paper: 770 co-authors (LIGO gravitational wave search, 2011)
  • 5-plus author papers: Became the dominant category by 2012
  • Citation advantage: Multi-author papers are cited more frequently relative to their publication rate

2025 Update

  • Average authors: 1.00 in 1827, 11.99 in 2024. Nearly 12x increase.
  • Single-author papers: 100 percent in 1827, 3.5 percent in 2024. Effectively extinct.
  • 5-plus author papers: 0 percent in 1827, 61.7 percent in 2024. Now the dominant form.
  • Largest paper: 1,782 co-authors in 2024. Mega-collaborations now routine.
  • Inflection point: 1996 marked when papers with 5-plus authors surpassed single-author papers.
  • Research population: Grew faster than paper output from 1960 onward, showing increased specialisation and complexity.
