Authorship in Astronomical Literature

1827 to 2024 (Last updated 2026)

Analysis of authorship patterns in astronomical literature from 1827 to 2024. Dataset: papers across eight major journals (MNRAS, ApJ, A and A, AJ, PASP, Nature Astronomy, ApJ Letters, PASA). 427,735+ unique authors.

Updated monthly via automated pipeline. Open source. Data freely available.

GitHub CSV Data

This dataset spans 199 years, from 1827 to 2024. Analysis covers eight major journals: MNRAS, ApJ, A and A, AJ, PASP, Nature Astronomy, ApJ Letters, and PASA. 427,735+ unique researchers across the dataset.

Three dates matter. In 1960, co-authorship began its acceleration. By 1996, papers with five or more authors had overtaken single-author work. As of the latest analysis in 2024, single-author papers represent just 3.5 percent of the total.

Observations

1827 to 1960

  • Authorship remained at approximately 1.0 authors per paper
  • Single-author papers comprised the vast majority of all publications

1960 to 1996

  • Co-authorship began increasing from 1960 onward
  • By 1996, papers with 5-plus authors overtook single-author papers for the first time

1996 to 2024

  • Average authors per paper: 12.0 (2024)
  • Single-author papers: 3.5 percent of total (2024)
  • Papers with 5-plus authors: 61.7 percent of total (2024)
  • Maximum co-authors on a single paper: 1,782 (2024)
  • Total unique authors across dataset: 427,735
  • Total papers across dataset: 153,330
